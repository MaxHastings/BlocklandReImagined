## 0. The contract

v0.2.6 is the last release whose main purpose is growing the foundational
NPC brain. After it, most new bot competence comes from declared
mechanics, measured capabilities, the shared planning and worth
mechanisms, and fixes to those, not from another authored behaviour. When
a bot cannot do X, the allowed answers are: the content needs to declare
X; a shared mechanism has a bug; or X is a new general affordance the
platform does not represent yet, added once for players and bots alike.
The answer that is not allowed is a behaviour that knows what X is called.
In one sentence: a bot reasons only from what it perceives, what content
declares, what its movement was measured to do and what it observed
happen; it chooses among ordinary controls with one chooser; content
expressed in the platform's vocabulary needs no bot-specific code.

**Success is measured by what is deleted as much as by what is added.**
The fudge factors, the flat push worth, the Team mover, the own-goal
geometry rules, every content-name read, and the duplicated weapon
readings each go when the mechanism that replaces them lands. A new
mechanism that leaves its old special case beside it has not landed.

Two mechanisms are new. Everything visible in this release is a case of
one of them, not a feature of its own:

- **Where to stand** (position worth). A bounded set of reachable spots,
  each scored by what it enables: a clear shot with the weapon in hand,
  inside its band, clear of allies, how exposed it is, what the route
  costs. The chooser picks. Firing spots, stepping out of an ally's line
  of fire, cover when hurt, a safe place to fire a rocket and an angle on
  a ball are all this.
- **What it would do** (consequence worth). An attack is valued by its
  predicted effect, not a flat number: where a push lands the target and
  what is there; what a blast clears; what a hold or release changes.
  Ledge pushes, clearing an opponent off an objective and a push toward
  the right goal are all this.

The rest of the round makes those two honest: measured movement (what the
body can really do), declared mechanics (what the item really does, fuse
and bounce included), one visibility question (what it can really see),
and one connected inner state (why it is inconsistent the way a person is).

## 2. Each item: fit, cost, risk

### Item 3: One visibility question

- **Today.** `Simulation::sight` (`crates/sim/src/simulation.rs:1664`)
  treats every brick with `raycast` as a wall, so glass hides a player
  completely. Sight reach is the kind's `sight` whatever the weather. A
  scaled player is aimed at by fixed eye and chest points.
- **Fit.** Every bot sight query already goes through one place
  (`bots/sightlines.rs`, `Session::bot_sees`). Three inputs join it, all
  engine data:
  - a brick's paint is a palette colour with an alpha
    (`crates/world/src/model.rs:487`); a colour under full alpha does not
    block sight. `clear_path` (`bots/combat.rs:981`) keeps treating it as
    solid, since shots stop on glass. The gap between "I see you" and "I
    can't hit you" is what item 4 exists for, which is why 3 and 4 ship
    together;
  - the environment's fog and visible distance
    (`crates/content/src/atmosphere.rs:121-126`, server-side data) cap
    sight reach, so bots lose you in fog where you lose them;
  - the target's real body size (its scale, already read for melee reach
    at `combat.rs:176`) sizes the aim points and the aim error, so a tiny
    player is harder to hit and a giant easier.
- **Cost.** One filter and one cap in the sight ray; no new rays.
- **Risk.** Bots in a glass box see out and cannot shoot out; without
  item 4 they would stand and stare. Ship 3 only with 4. Darkness is not
  included: the server has no lighting, so "can it see in the dark" would
  be a new simulation built for bots, which the scope rule forbids.

### Item 4: Where to stand, with firing spots as its case

- **Today.** Fight is chosen while the enemy is in sight within the band
  (`behaviour.rs:157`). The hand-combat chooser then looks for a shot from
  where the bot stands; a blocked path drops the candidate
  (`combat.rs:625-633`). With no candidate the bot keeps Fight, strafes
  and does not fire. Nothing moves it toward a line.
- **Fit.** The Stance mover (`act.rs`, `Mover::Stance`) already decides
  where a fighter stands. Give it a scored choice of standing spots: the
  spot it is on plus a few neighbours on the walk grid (a step either way,
  a step in or out, and, where measured reach says it lands, a hop up or a
  jet up). One score per spot, through the same chooser (`Mind::pick`,
  a new `Domain::Spot` beside aim and route):
  - worth: the best shot from there (the existing candidate scoring,
    `tactics::suitability`, run with that origin);
  - cost: the route to it from measured reach (item 1), through
    `route`'s own edge costs;
  - exposure: how many enemies in sight see that spot, from the shared
    ray budget, weighted by how hurt the bot is (the health share the
    urgent gate already reads).
  The exposure term is what makes cover and retreat fall out: a healthy bot
  wants the shot and does not mind being seen; a hurt one prefers a spot
  with a worse shot and nobody looking. No Retreat behaviour, no cover
  points, no map tags.
- **Cost.** About 6 spot samples per planning turn, 1 ray each, on the
  ordinary ray budget (`sightlines.rs`, 128 a tick shared). Cache a spot's
  answer while neither end moves half a unit, like other sight answers.
  Check against `bot_think_time_16`.
- **Risk.** Term creep. Three terms, all from data the bot already has, and
  no new dial: surprise's strength bands it like every other choice. If a
  fourth term is wanted, that is a design note first. Second risk: a bot
  that dances between two spots; the hold rule covers it as it does aim.

### Item 5: Loop 3: stock behaviour as data

- **Today.** `crates/weapons/src/runtime/stock.rs` names the spear, key,
  balls, skis and the rest. The capabilities plan's loop 3 moves that table
  into the importer so an Add-On weapon can declare "charges like the
  spear" or "runs the hammer" in data.
- **Fit.** Already designed and reviewed (plan §3 and §9). Needs a content
  regeneration on Max's PC, so it merges through the PC thread, and the
  proof is a replay recorded on main played back on the branch with the
  same checksums.
- **Decided (capabilities lane, 06:47Z).** The sports port moves to data
  with the rest and `stock.rs` is deleted, not kept as a documented port;
  the native `OnFire` state script wraps `HostTool` rather than standing
  beside it.
- **Why it is before grenades.** Without it a grenade would be told by its
  cook script's name. With it, "has a fuse" and "bounces" are fields the
  importer fills for stock and Add-On items alike.
- **Risk.** It touches `stock.rs`, the importer and the client
  `Equipment` map. One lane owns all three.

### Item 6: Timed throws (grenades)

- **Today.** An image with `cook` is refused by the native reader
  (`tactics.rs:401`, `SecondaryEffects`), so a grenade is "no weapon" to
  the bot (`fire.rs`). It will not throw it, or throws it as a slow rocket
  with no fuse. Add-On weapon packs are full of them.
- **Fit.** One more `Delivery` kind beside Ray, Projectile and Contact:
  `Timed { flight, fuse_ticks, bounces }`, filled from the projectile's
  `max_bounces` and bounce data and the image's `Cook::fuse_ticks`
  (`weapons/src/lib.rs:709, 1983`). The arc solver already steps the real
  projectile for curved shots (`clear_path`, `PATH_TICKS`); it gains
  bounces by continuing that same step after a hit, so there is no second
  physics model. The aim it wants is "where it lies when the fuse ends,
  within the splash radius of the target", solved like the feet and
  surface aims the splash chooser already offers. `safe_blast` already
  says whether that lands on the bot or an ally.
- **Children.** A projectile that throws out smaller ones
  (`children`, refused today) is accepted with the children at zero
  damage, and the blast's danger zone (`safe_blast`) widened by how far
  they fly, so the bot stands clear of them without pretending to know
  what they do.
- **Cost.** A bounce solve is a few more projectile steps per candidate.
  Bound bounces to what the data allows and the fuse to `PATH_TICKS`.
- **Risk.** Fuse data on stock content is thin, so the proving ground is an
  Add-On pack from Max's set (the project folder has two). Cooking (holding
  the pin so it bursts in the air) is a second trigger style; leave it out
  of this version and say so.

### Item 7: What it would do, with ledge pushes as its case

- **Today.** A push-only attack is worth a flat `PUSH_WORTH` of 10
  (`tactics.rs:11, 605`). A broom shove near a cliff and one in a flat
  field score the same.
- **Fit.** The worth becomes the harm at the predicted landing. Two inputs
  from two lanes:
  - the landing: where the shove's impulse (the capability's `push`) sends
    the target, from the measured-moves seam (`reach.rs` measures the
    actor; a shove is one more measured flight);
  - the harm, read only from data the engine already interprets, with no
    second event reader for bots:
    - the body's falling damage: the same `min_impact_speed` and
      `Armor::onImpact` formula `step_combat` applies
      (`session/combat.rs:2415-2480`), pulled into one function both
      call, gated by the same mini-game `can_damage(Falling)` and host
      `falling_damage` setting, so a game with falling damage off makes
      a shove worth nothing;
    - kill bricks only through one query of the events system (the same
      one the objective planner's projection uses, `bots/objectives/
      project.rs`); if that query cannot say a touch kills, the landing
      counts fall damage only;
    - no out-of-world kill is invented for bots: a landing below the
      world is worth whatever the fall rule says, nothing more.
    Nothing reads a map or a brick name.
  Harm feeds the same `damage` slot the scorer already has, so a shove that
  kills is worth the target's health and one that lands them on a step is
  worth about nothing. The same landing prediction answers the soccer
  questions the contest code answers by hand today (a push toward the
  wrong sensor, `bots.md` "own goals"): the landing crosses a sensor the
  rules say scores against the team, so it is worth less than nothing. Those
  hand rules are deleted only once the prediction covers them. The shot
  judge already observes outcomes (`Mind::outcome`, `surprise.rs:603`);
  a push is judged by it the same way (did the target move where
  predicted), so a wrong prediction loses effectiveness and the bot tries
  something else. No separate Outcome record is built for this. The same number makes a bot *avoid* standing where
  it would be shoved: a firing spot's exposure can carry it, for free.
- **Risk.** Fight balance on Close Quarters and Slopes shifts toward
  shoves. That is the point ("destruction over smoothness"), but check the
  soccer kits still play the ball (the broom test in `bot_soccer_match.rs`).

### Item 8: Small human-feel items

All tiny, no new state, each reusing a signal the bot already has (from
`bots/human-variation-candidates.md`, the ones not yet done):

- hit mid-strafe, turn the other way (`strafe_leg` takes `parted`;
  pass "hurt this tick" in beside it, `bots.rs:605`);
- missing, fire shorter bursts (`cadence::bursting` takes the weapon's
  effectiveness as a factor);
- bored, fidget more (idle look drift scaled by the play boredom);
- social goofs near people, solo goofs alone (scale the existing flavour
  weights by whether `Pause.player` is near).

Do these last, in one small PR, after the big three are in, so the
baseline for judging them is clean.

### Item 9: Mood, the first two steps

- **Why in.** The second opinion is right that this is the piece that
  makes the humanizing dice one actor: hurt raises alert, which speeds the
  turn, steadies the aim after a moment and stops the goofing, together.
  Today each of those rolls on its own.
- **Fit.** `bots/mood-design-note.md` steps 1 and 2 only: compute the four
  values from signals the brain already has (hurt, sight, boredom,
  effectiveness, health), show them in F3, then let the "does" stage (turn
  speed, aim steadiness, burst pauses) read them. Steps 3 to 5 (the chooser
  reads it, alertness comes from it, kinds become temperaments) are v0.2.7.
- **Why last and as its own preview.** It changes how every bot moves its
  head and hand. Max plays the first preview (items 1 to 8) against
  tonight's baseline, then a second preview with mood on. That is how a
  change in feel gets blamed on the right thing.
- **What goes.** Each step removes the dice it replaces in the same
  change: step 1 removes nothing (read-only), step 2 removes the
  reaction jitter draw (`perception.rs:307`), the flat turn speed scale
  and the seeded burst-pause share where the mood now sets them. Nothing
  dormant: a value nothing reads is not shipped past step 1.
- **Numbers.** Rest values, settle rates and reader caps are named
  constants in code, not `bots.json` settings. Kinds as temperaments
  (step 5) is the only thing that would make them data, and it is not in
  this version.
- **Risk.** A runaway loop (frustrated misses more, so more frustrated).
  Every value settles back to rest at a fixed rate and each reader's
  effect is capped; a multi-seed never-invariant checks no value pins at
  0 or 1. Fairness stays behind the one perception dial.

## 3. How each item avoids the special-case trap

| Item | The trap | Why it is not one |
|---|---|---|
| See-through | "glass bricks" by name | reads paint alpha, a field every brick has |
| Firing spots | "if wall then jump" | spots are options with one score; the chooser picks; hop and jet come from measured reach |
| Loop 3 | a list of item names in code | the list moves to the importer's data, out of the runtime |
| Grenades | "if HE grenade" | fuse and bounce are projectile and image fields; any Add-On item with them works |
| Ledge pushes | tagging deadly spots on maps | harm comes from the body's fall rule, event-wired kill bricks and the world bound |
| Human-feel | new timers and coins | each scales an existing term by an existing signal |
| Fog, target size | "dark map" or "tiny player" checks | sight reach from the environment's own distances; aim points from the body's own scale |
| Mood | a personality system | four numbers fed by signals the brain already has, read as one multiply each; replaces dice, adds no knob |

Rules each PR is checked against before merge: no behaviour added; no
content name read; one score per option through `Mind::pick`; nothing on
its own timer (cadence beats only); every number a named constant or data;
no new dial in `bots.json`; the F3 readout shows the new term.

## 5. Stretch: horses

Bots take only `Family::Wheeled` vehicles (`interactions.rs:155, 349`)

with a rider; its edges are walk edges at its own speed and footprint.

## 6. Cut from this version, and why

- **Mood steps 3 to 5** (the chooser reads it, alertness from it, kinds
  as temperaments). Steps 1 and 2 are in (item 9); the rest changes what
  bots choose and waits until the first two have been played.
- **Darkness in sight.** The server has no lighting; building one for
  bots is new simulation, which the scope rule forbids. Fog is in because
  its data already exists.
- **Planes and boats** (the dogfight save). A flying vehicle needs its own
  measured flight model and a 3D route graph; that is the locomotion seam
  again, but large. v0.2.7, measured the same way jets were.
- **Goal defending and passing as their own work.** Both risk the "soccer
  goalie" special case. Where to stand (item 4) and what it would do
  (item 7) should start producing them for free: a spot between the threat
  and the thing the rules say the team protects scores on exposure and
  shot; a push that lands the ball near a teammate scores on consequence.
  If they do not fall out, that is a v0.2.7 design note, not a v0.2.6
  patch.
- **The other v0.2.5 leftovers** (objective commitment through the hold
  rule, one progress judge per step, the Gravity Gun throw as a hold and
  release, idle ball play on the shared push, leash-aware strolls). They
  are not separate projects any more. Each is tried as a case of the new
  mechanisms when its lane is in that code; one that does not fall out
  stays queued with a line saying why.
- **Cooking grenades.** A second trigger style on top of item 6; later.
- **Emotes after kills, grudges, confidence.** Each needs new state or a
  new feedback loop. They become one-line readers once mood exists.
- **The 13 test-only kind numbers as constants.** Code health a player
  cannot see; the tests need rewriting first.

## 7. The acceptance run

One synthetic package no production code knows the names of: an odd body
(different gravity, jump and jet numbers), an unknown projectile weapon
with bounce, fuse and splash, an unknown push weapon, a see-through solid
barrier, a loose objective body, raised platforms, a drop that hurts, and
a vehicle with unusual steering and brakes. Several bots in it, recorded,
replayed exactly, run under several seeds. The checks are absolutes only
(Max's testing rule): a bot gets up the platforms; sees someone through
the barrier; fires from somewhere; throws the fused weapon and it goes
off near the target at least once; pushes someone off the drop at least
once and never pushes a teammate off; moves the objective; and nothing is
stuck for the whole run. Shares are printed, not gated. This extends the
convergence test the capabilities plan already names (§6 there). The
capabilities lane writes the package and the test; the review thread
writes the spec first and judges the results; the other lanes hand over
their halves.

## 8. The second opinion, checked

Max shared a ChatGPT take on 2026-10-07. What I kept, changed or refused:

  grenades and ledge pushes as acceptance cases. That is the right frame

## 9. Risks across the whole round

- **Too much at once to playtest.** Tonight's preview is the baseline.
  Each PR records a replay of one of Max's saves on its own build. What
  replay proves: an odd moment becomes a case that reproduces exactly on
  that build, and an unintended divergence can be located. What it does
  not prove: that a behaviour change is better. Loop 3 and any "nothing
  changes" PR are proved by identical checksums; behaviour PRs are judged
  by the share report, the acceptance absolutes and Max's play.
- **Performance.** Firing spots and bounce solves add rays and steps. Both
  go on existing budgets, and `bot_think_time_16` gates every merge.
- **Content regeneration.** Loop 3 and any importer field change needs
  packs rebuilt on the PC; the PC thread merges those.
- **Lane overlap.** Three places, each with a rule:
  - `tactics.rs` `PUSH_WORTH` and the harm reader: one function handed
    from capabilities to fighting, agreed before the edit;
  - `combat.rs`: the fighting lane scores a spot by running the existing
    candidate chooser from another origin, and grenades add a candidate
    kind to the same chooser. The fighting lane merges first (its one
    change is adding an origin parameter); the capabilities lane merges
    main in before touching the candidate loop;
  - `reach.rs`: the fighting lane only calls it; the shove landing is
    written by the locomotion lane and merged before ledge pushes start.
- **The content tests on the known-failures list** (three that need a
  `blockhead_bot` install in the main checkout's content, owner "v0.2.6
  bot work") are the PC thread's: regenerate the main checkout's content
  once loop 3 merges, and take them off the list.
- **Scope creep.** The finish-line rule stands: a new finding goes in only
  if a player would hit it, it is in code being changed, or leaving it
  means a hack. Everything else gets one line in `bots/next-version.md`.
