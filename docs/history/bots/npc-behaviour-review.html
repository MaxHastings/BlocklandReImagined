How the bots behave

A full review of the v0.2.5 bots for Max: what makes them human, varied and surprising in play, and how each part works underneath. Read from branch claude/v0.2.5-s4s5bn at 2d45224 ("go explore"). Code references are under crates/sim/src/session/. I read the code only; nothing was run for this page.

The core idea. Every tick a bot looks at what it can see, hear and remember. It gives each thing it could do a score, picks the best or a near-best one, and sticks with it for a moment so it doesn't flicker. A single act step then turns that choice into the walk, the look and the buttons a player would press.

Where the human feel comes from. Mostly from what the bot knows: how alert it is, how long it has tracked you, what keeps failing, what bored it, what teammates are doing. A smaller part is a slow personal "mood" drift, and a remaining part is plain dice, mostly in idle play.

Where it's still thin. Which goof it does is still a dice roll. Dodges, weapon hand-offs and door clicks run on their own timers. Brick bots stroll to random spots. Searching spins like a turret. Stuck spots appear on big maps now that bots cross them. Most of this is already queued on the bot thread.

How a bot thinks, step by step

This runs every tick (120 times a second) for every bot, in step_bot (bots.rs:1623).

1

Perceive

It sees enemies within its sight distance and line of sight, through portals too (bots/sight.rs:85). There is no blind spot: its view cone only makes it react slower to someone outside it. It hears weapon fire up to 128 units away and roughly where (bots/hearing.rs:110), hears allies' warnings after a short delay (hearing.rs:68), and feels hits, knowing only roughly where an unseen shooter is.

2

Remember

What it learned is kept as dated evidence: who, where, when, and until when (Knowledge, bots.rs:277). It never peeks at where an unseen enemy is now. Search memory works through spots to check from that evidence (bots/search_memory.rs). Explore remembers which areas it has visited lately (bots/explore.rs:55).

3

Score every option

It has ten things it can be doing: Carry, Interact, Fight, Arm, Chase, Search, Return, Objective, Explore, Wander. Each gets a score from what's true now (bots/behaviour.rs, score). For example, Fight scores 0.8 inside its weapon's range and fades past it, Chase 0.6, an objective 0.65 (0.92 when carrying), Search 0.4, Explore 0.2 and Wander 0.1. An enemy's score is scaled by how much it threatens the bot or its play (bot_menace, bots/contest.rs:37). The kind's weights in bots.json can turn options up, down or off.

4

Team terms

Each bot posts what it's doing (where, at whom, which seat, its line of fire) as an "intent" (bots/claims.rs, bots/team.rs:300). Before choosing, each bot adjusts its scores from its allies' intents. Doubling up on the same target or spot costs a little, and taking a seat an ally offers scores more. Standing in an ally's line of fire costs more. Its objective is worth more the further its side is behind (pressed, team.rs:168).

5

Mood: drift, boredom, effectiveness, copying

Before the pick, each score is nudged by four things (Mind::pick, bots/surprise.rs:485):

  • Drift: each bot's slow, wandering liking for each option. Each bot starts at a different point, and it changes over about 90 s.
  • Boredom: whatever it's doing grows dull, and the boredom fades over about 20 s once it stops.
  • Effectiveness: options that failed (shots dodged, no damage, stuck) lose value and recover over about 30 s.
  • Copy: a bonus for what it saw work for a teammate.

Drift and boredom together can move a score at most 15 %, so only nearly tied options swap. None of this applies when it's urgent (low health, or just hit up close) or carrying the objective (Gate, surprise.rs:203).

6

Choose, and hold the choice

One rule decides for every choice point: behaviour, weapon, aim point, flank route, goof-or-play, move and dodge (Hold::choose, bots/behaviour.rs:297). A new choice is held for at least 0.5 s. After that, a rival must beat it by 10 % to take over. Some things cut in at once: an urgent hit, an objective appearing or being dropped, a new target, the target dying, or the held option becoming impossible.

7

Plan the way

The route planner finds a path on the walk grid, including swimming, jetting, portals and driving (bot_path, bots.rs:1402). The objective planner works out what the game's own rules ask and how to do it (bots/objectives/turn.rs, bots/planning.rs). It reads the authored event rows and package rules; it never matches map or mode names.

8

Act: one place turns choices into controls

Everything that wants to move the bot proposes. In order: route, push, fight stance, jet, swim, team, goof, dodge, stand. One fixed priority decides, and safety goes last and can only take away: no stumbling into portals, a step round vehicles, and never off a ledge that hurts (bots/act.rs). Aim and the trigger are still set in step_bot and bot_act (bots.rs:1191); moving them into the act step is in progress.

9

Execute like a player

It sends the same move input a player sends: forward, strafe, yaw, pitch, jump, crouch, jet, trigger. Every game rule applies to it unchanged. If it's getting nowhere, one stuck judge hops once, then replans, then gives up on the goal (bots.rs:3001).

Every behaviour a player can see

For each one: what sets it off, what makes it vary, and whether that variation follows the situation or is dice.

Fighting

Spotting and reacting

contextsmall jitter
Trigger
An enemy comes into sight or hits it.
Varies by
How alert it was: quick when fighting, slower when strolling or goofing. Slower again from outside its view cone, where it also turns toward you more slowly. Hit from behind, its look freezes until it reacts. The delay has ±30 % jitter. perception.rs:284-311, 489-560

Aim and turning

contextsmooth wobble
Trigger
An enemy in sight.
Varies by
Turning speeds up into big turns, eases out and overshoots slightly on a fast flick. Its miss size is wide when it first sees you and tightens as it keeps tracking. It is wider after a slow reaction, and stays wider for a target crossing fast or far away. The miss direction is a smooth wobble that never settles. Lead on moving targets is roughly right and drifts ±15 %. bots.rs:802-816, 2630; perception.rs:476, 570

Where it aims

contextdrift
Trigger
Firing.
Varies by
Torso, feet or a nearby surface is a real choice. Splash weapons aim at the feet. An aim point that keeps missing loses value, and drift and boredom can switch between close options. surprise.rs:170; combat.rs:778; surprise.rs:1092

Firing rhythm

timer
Trigger
Aim on target, path clear, no ally in the way.
Varies by
Each bot taps on its own beat and fires in bursts with pauses on its own seeded cycle, so bots never fire in unison. The rhythm doesn't react to hits or misses yet. cadence.rs:82; bots.rs:1191

Picking a weapon

contextdrift
Trigger
A fight starts, or the situation changes: range, allies in the line, blast too close.
Varies by
Each weapon is rated for the shot from its item data (range, splash, speed, damage), then goes through the same hold rule. A weapon that isn't landing hits loses value. combat.rs:734-765; tactics.rs; surprise.rs:1082

Stance: strafing and footwork

seeded rhythmcoin flip
Trigger
Fighting within weapon range.
Varies by
Ranged bots strafe one way for 1.75 to 5.25 s, then turn back 70 % of the time. They turn early at a wall, a ledge or an ally, and lean in slowly. Melee bots step in, back and aside, then plant, on their own cycle. Strafe length and turning are dice; they don't respond to being shot. bots.rs:560-584, 840

Chasing and flanking

contextdrift
Trigger
An enemy in sight but out of range.
Varies by
Straight at them, or wide round the left or right. This is a real choice with effectiveness: a route that got stuck loses value. It gives up on enemies it can't reach. surprise.rs:895, 1039; bots.rs:3001

Dodge and crouch

contextown timers
Trigger
A projectile whose predicted path (speed, fall, blast radius) meets its body, or being hit from range.
Varies by
It can hop, strafe off the line (where there's floor) or jet up if it has the fuel, chosen with boredom so the same dodge gets stale. A crouch is also possible. It's held by private timers, not the hold rule; moving it under the hold rule is in progress. extras.rs:322-440

Fight or keep playing?

context
Trigger
An enemy in sight while it has an objective, like a ball.
Varies by
An enemy counts in full if it hit the bot, the play names it, or it's at or contesting the ball. It counts 0.75 if armed and elsewhere, and 0.25 if unarmed. So a bot shoulders someone off the ball but doesn't drop a delivery for a passer-by. contest.rs:37
Finding the fight

Searching

contextspin
Trigger
It lost sight of an enemy, or was hit by someone unseen.
Varies by
It goes where they were last seen, then through nearby spots they could have gone, and skips spots it can't reach. At each spot it spins its view at a steady rate, which reads robotic. search_memory.rs:72; bots.rs:2678

Following gunfire

context
Trigger
A rules bot with nothing to do hears a weapon up to 128 units away, scaled by loudness.
Varies by
It knows the spot less exactly the farther the sound, and keeps it in mind for as long as the walk there takes. Melee weapons make no sound that carries. hearing.rs:110

Exploring (new)

contextsmall variety
Trigger
A rules bot with nothing in sight, nothing remembered and no objective, while enemies are playing.
Varies by
It looks a way off in each open direction and picks the place most worth checking: not visited lately (90 s), near where enemies were last known, and not where a teammate is already heading, plus a little seeded variety. Reported on Max's saves: wander dropped from 79-89 % to 4-6 %. explore.rs:112

Ally warnings

context
Trigger
An ally sees an enemy or is hit, and the kind warns allies.
Varies by
Allies within the warner's sight hear it after 0.25 to 1 s, scaled by how alert they are, and the spot they get is a little off. So a squad doesn't snap round as one. perception.rs:520-545; hearing.rs:68
Objectives and ball games

Playing the game's objective

context
Trigger
The game's own rules (event rows, package rules) say what wins, and the planner finds a grounded way to do it: walk somewhere, press something, push or carry a body there, beat someone.
Varies by
The planner ranks routes by cost. Steps that fail cool down and get replanned. The objective is worth more as its side falls behind. No map or mode names are used. objectives/turn.rs; planning.rs; team.rs:168

Pushing a ball (soccer)

context
Trigger
An objective to get a body (the ball) into a goal area.
Varies by
It comes round behind the ball on the line to the goal and steers around its own goal from the game's sensors. If the ball is moving and an opponent contests it, it meets the ball ahead along its real motion. physical_objectives.rs:840-858; contest.rs:83-146

Headers and chesting the ball

context
Trigger
The body it's pushing is up off the ground, higher than a step and lower than its head, right in front of it.
Varies by
It jumps into it. The jump is only used where it would land on floor. physical_objectives.rs:850-858

Clearing and covering

context
Trigger
A teammate has the ball and an opponent stands at it.
Varies by
With a pushing tool (its item data says it shoves), it knocks the opponent aside and calls "I got him, go!". Otherwise it covers, standing behind and to the side of the play, ready to take over. Today this swaps the bot's choice after it's made; folding it into the scoring is in progress. contest.rs:185, 281; bots.rs ~2270

Hammering a body along

context
Trigger
It holds the engine's built-in hammer and the body is one the hammer may hit.
Varies by
It hits the body toward the goal on the push approach. This one is keyed to the built-in hammer, which is under review. physical_objectives.rs:506, 860-885
Items, vehicles and things in the world

Arming and upgrading

contextdrift
Trigger
Empty-handed with an enemy about, or a better weapon in sight.
Varies by
Empty-handed, it goes for any weapon at once. Armed, it weighs an upgrade by how much better it is, minus the walk there and the risk of turning from a nearby enemy. Weapons it can't reach are passed over for a while. arming.rs:74, 178

Grab, carry and throw

contexttimer
Trigger
Its tool holds something, like the Gravity Gun.
Varies by
It carries it to open sky, keeping it off the floor and under the roof, then flings it with a swing. If it can't find open space within 6 s, it throws anyway. bots.rs:186-205, carry section

Driving and crewing vehicles

contexttimer
Trigger
The route or objective needs a vehicle, or a teammate offers a seat.
Varies by
Drivers follow route legs with gears, wait a moment for crew, and keep a chase within 96 units of where they boarded. Gunners fight from their seat only with a weapon that reaches. Passengers without one get out. Dismounts happen on each bot's own beat. interactions.rs:1164-1300; CREW_WAIT 18

Ride-alongs and idle pushing

context
Trigger
No enemy about. A teammate's vehicle has a free seat, or a loose body is near a player.
Varies by
These are low-priority "Interact" options, so anything with a purpose outranks them. Seats an ally offers score higher. interactions.rs:503; extras.rs header

Doors

on the wayfor fun
Trigger
A door (a brick that opens and closes on click) is in the way to its goal. Or, at a pause, a door in sight.
Varies by
It clicks doors in its way when needed. Clicking one for fun runs on a beat and rest timer, not through the chooser. It never presses a builder's buttons (win, reset, teleport). extras.rs:181-214, 521-541, 609-620

Handing a weapon to a teammate

contexttimer
Trigger
It has a spare weapon and sees an unarmed teammate.
Varies by
It walks up, faces them and drops the spare their way. It runs on a beat with a 20 s rest, and is switched on and off by the teamwork dial. Moving it into the chooser is in progress. extras.rs:670-747
Idle life and goofing

Whether to goof

context
Trigger
No enemy in sight, on its feet, and not carrying the objective.
Varies by
Playing grows boring, twice as fast with nothing to do. A goof is worth most in a lull and little with action close by. Bots nearby goofing make it likelier (social pull). Doing a goof relieves the boredom. surprise.rs:674-787; team.rs:428-546

Which goof, and how

dice
Trigger
Goofing wins.
Varies by
The goofs are: stare, emote, hop, circle, detour, look round, crouch, spray paint, another tool, drop its weapon, light, door. Which one is a roulette over fixed weights, filtered by what's possible, plus a bonus for goofs it saw others do. The details are coin flips too: which emote, how long, walk up close or not, gait. This is the biggest "reads random" gap; making it a scored choice is in progress. surprise.rs:754-778

Emotes

dice
Trigger
An emote goof, or the kind's own once-per-life pose (zombie arms).
Varies by
One of love, hate, confusion or alarm, at random. Nothing ties an emote to a moment yet. The bot thread has been asked to tie emotes to signals the bot already has: surprise, frustration from failing, confusion when stuck, playfulness. surprise.rs:328; bots.rs:1156

Glances

context
Trigger
While idle: a blast, a loud sound, something fast, someone staring at it, or someone nearby while it strolls.
Varies by
Chance and reach come from how big, loud, fast or close it is. Glances are short and cool down. perception.rs:1-30, 694

Strolling

dicetimer
Trigger
Nothing else to do.
Varies by
Brick bots walk to a random point within 12 units of their brick every 2 to 6 s, with the look drifting a few degrees as they walk. Rules bots explore instead (above). Reusing explore for brick bots is in progress. bots.rs:667; perception.rs:593

Walking home

context
Trigger
A brick bot strays past its stroll area.
Varies by
The urge grows over 4 units past the edge, and the hold rule stops it flickering at the edge. It doesn't head home while an objective it's working on is cooling down. behaviour.rs, Return
Team play

Spreading out and taking roles

context
Trigger
Allies' posted intents.
Varies by
Going after the same target or spot as an ally costs a little more for each extra ally. Taking a seat an ally offers scores more. Standing in an ally's line of fire costs more. A fighter steps out of where a teammate's weapon will hit. team.rs side_terms; bots.rs, Team mover

Callouts

context
Trigger
A teammate's intent changed its choice, or it's clearing an opponent.
Varies by
At most one callout every 30 s. Each reason has one fixed line ("Coming!", "Moving!", "I got him, go!"), so lines repeat word for word. team.rs:603; bots.json

Mood and copying

context
Trigger
It sees nearby players (either side) goofing, or an option working for a teammate.
Varies by
Goofs get likelier, and the same goof more so. It reads what players visibly do, never chat. Options it saw work get a fading bonus. team.rs:428-580; surprise.rs:621-655

Pressure

context
Trigger
Its side is behind in the round.
Varies by
The objective is worth more the further behind it is. team.rs:168
Body, kind and safety

Looks and names

seeded
Trigger
Spawning.
Varies by
Each bot gets its own seeded colours, face and decal from the server's avatar pack, and a first name from its kind's list that nobody else has. looks.rs; lifecycle.rs:172, 411

Respawning

seeded
Trigger
Death.
Varies by
A seeded wait after the game allows a respawn (0.3-1.5 s for rules bots, 0.75-1.75 s for brick bots), so bots killed together don't come back together. Choices reset each life; its liking for options carries over. bots.rs:1066

Kinds: sharks, zombies

context
Trigger
The kind's data.
Varies by
A swimmer stays in its water and suffocates out of it. A biter's hit can turn its victim into its kind for a life. All of this comes from the Add-On's data, not code per creature. bots.rs:1156; fire.rs:325-361

Ledges, portals and getting stuck

context
Trigger
Every step.
Varies by
It never walks off a fall that would hurt, judged from its body's fall-damage data, and the safety step has the last word. It never stumbles into a portal off its route. If it's stuck, it hops, then replans, then drops the goal. act.rs:150; interactions.rs:928; bots.rs:3001

In progress on the bot thread right now

From the bot thread's status and the coordinator's latest notes to it (as of 19:35 UTC):

  • Act step: moving aim and the trigger in, so every control comes from one place. This includes the stuck hop, the goof spray trigger and better hop checks.
  • Clearing and covering: folding them into the scored choice, so there's one way to go after an opponent at the ball.
  • Goofs: each goof becomes a scored option driven by what the bot perceives. Emotes express signals the bot already has (surprise, frustration, confusion, playfulness) through a small data table, with boredom as the cooldown.
  • Dodges, hand-offs and door clicks: moving them through the one chooser and the act step.
  • Strolling: brick bots use the explore scorer.
  • Firing: bursts respond to hits and misses through the existing effectiveness signal.
  • Stuck spots: one root fix for Close Quarters and Afghanistan.
  • Spray can and hammer: make each name check general, or give a one-line reason why not.
  • Before handing over: merge latest main, rerun quick matches on Max's saves, send the tip.
