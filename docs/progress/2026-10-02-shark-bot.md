# Shark: an Add-On's own model as a player body, and the Shark port

- Import Add-On: a `PlayerData`'s `shapeFile` that converted in its Add-On
  becomes the archetype's `model` (`<package>:asset/<file>`).
- Client: `AvatarAssets::load_bodies` loads every enabled package's
  archetype models of that form (shape, its own sequences, its materials'
  textures from the package's `content.json`); `body_mesh` draws it in
  place of the Blockhead, painting objects named as colour slots and
  hiding the rest. The horse is now one of these bodies. A body that does
  not load is an Add-On health problem and its players stay Blockheads.
  Test: `an_add_on_player_model_draws_as_its_body`. The real shark.dts
  loaded and painted (head, chest, arms; helmet and visor hidden) in a
  throwaway check against the real import.
- Port `bot_shark` (partial): swims (`moves: swim`), bites for this copy's
  35, painted its first colour with white fins, dies after 9 s out of
  water in a mini-game. Its script zeroes every speed; chosen here: 10/5/8
  in water, a slow flop on land. Test: `bot_holes.rs` on a CC0 stand-in.
- Bundle: Bot_Shark (off by default). The release build needs
  Bot_Shark.zip in `--search` (in /mnt/project-files).

Not ported yet: the mouth grab (hold a player, kill after 5 s), random
colours and Cool Shark, the `swim` animation in water (v20 holds root
there; the Shark's own loop plays `swim`), the hole brick hiding itself,
and the shark's large bounding box (it collides as a Blockhead).

Checked: addon-import (155 passed), client lib (384 passed), workspace
clippy -D warnings clean.
