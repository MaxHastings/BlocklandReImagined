# v0.2.1 playtest checklist

Start with the [creator guide](PLAYTEST.md). Use a copy of your save. These are
experiments to mutate, not polished game modes. Interactive feel is your test;
headless and offscreen checks cannot establish it.

## Creator tools: make everything ambiguous

1. Place **Switch door** and **Team door** on Slate. Their panels should be tall,
   solid ordinary bricks. Open, close, change teams, then add a key variable and
   delayed close. Add a second identical door with a different name. Keep a
   plain unguarded event alongside the complicated one: does it still feel easy?
2. Place **Soccer** with Steel Ball enabled. It has two identical ball spawners.
   The scoring ball is selected by **Object → Spawned by**, using the named
   spawner. Push the other ball into a goal: it should not score or reset through
   those rows. Inspect and change the condition to accept that ball instead.
   Rename/delete a spawner, reset a ball, and duplicate the contraption.
3. Combine checkpoints, puzzle switches and the hill. Make a shared variable
   unlock a team door. Rename one course's state key to separate it from a copy.
   Try two players at once, a player changing teams during a delay, and Reset
   with pending actions. IF reads current state when the delayed row becomes due.
4. Try both duplicators before and after **Change Map**. Copy a brick whose
   rendering and collision are off. Its placement preview should remain visible;
   planting should preserve its authored flags. Check that tool settings survive.
5. Open Wrench, Events, Detection region, Explain saved, MiniGames, Admin and
   Options at your normal resolution/UI scale. Test long names and several IFs.
   The guarded row reads trigger/delay → IF → DO. Admin should recover authority
   rather than leave host operations permanently disabled after a map change.

## Bots: ordinary rules, ordinary controls

Enable **Blockhead Bot** in **Start Game → Add-Ons**, create your MiniGame, and
plant an ordinary Vehicle Spawn brick with Blockhead Bot selected. Its bots use
the builder's MiniGame. They now receive truthful physical
**Player** and **MiniGame** context when the input offers those targets. They do
not get a fabricated connected **Client**. Touch still produces `onBotTouch`,
not a fake `onPlayerTouch`.

Build a small course with `onRegionEnter` rows targeting Player. Use Player
variables as progress, conditions for ordered checkpoints, and `winRound` on the
last checkpoint. Alternatively make four activation buttons set different
MiniGame variables and guard a final win on all four. Bots should approach and
activate/enter the actual bricks, observe the real progress, and replan. They
can compose supported activation, region and bot-touch rules; arbitrary scripts
are not inferred from their names.

Mutate the rules after the bot starts. Shuffle button order, add a delay, require
repeated region entry, put a checkpoint more than 150 units away, or make the
nearest objective inaccessible while leaving an alternative. Add a hostile bot
so combat interrupts progress. **Explain saved** includes a small NPC status or
unsupported reason for related rules. A relevant relay, unknown Add-On effect
or variable-change reaction can make the objective unsupported; that is safer
than predicting only its favorable rows.

Planning has an observable ceiling: four independent activation latches compose
and win in the measured fixture; eight independent latches exceed the current
search budget. Ordered checkpoint races work, including three complete laps.
Do not assume every larger combination is solved automatically. Record whether
the reason in Explain makes this limit understandable or frustrating.

Combat tests should include several available weapons, moving targets, allies
near the target, a wall behind the target, and a low ceiling. Supported native
weapons use authored trajectories, reach, ammo and charge behavior. The final
shot is checked after player movement, against its actual launch direction.
Unsupported script-driven weapons retain their existing behavior; this is not
a claim that every weapon/tool already has a tactical model.

Test a tank with two allied bots and a vacant elevated gunner seat. A passenger
can promote into the useful seat through normal seat switching. Compare melee
jet pursuit, building entry and recovery after a teammate blocks a corridor.

## Visuals, stress and remaining uncertainties

- Enable Shark and Zombie bots, spawn both, disable/re-enable the package, and
  change maps. Reloaded body assets should remain the correct model. Shark's
  complete original collision and behavior parity is still unfinished.
- Walk and drive slowly through portals in third person, especially halfway
  through and with a wall behind the portal. Look for boom collapse, one-frame
  reversed views and recrossing. Repeat both directions with a raised camera.
- Recreate the Bedroom battle: eight zombies against eight Blockheads, then
  change MiniGame equipment from Gun to Gravity Gun during combat. Also try
  Beta City 16 with rockets and spears. Record the weapon, map, bot count and
  approximate moment of any hang. The original Windows NaN crash has not been
  reproduced or causally fixed; improved crash reports and retained matching
  build symbols are intended to make another occurrence actionable.

Bots do **not** yet plan exact-ball delivery, hookshot routes, arbitrary Add-On
objectives, cooperative actor stacking or general team role assignments. Actor
support and climbing were proved through real controls, but discovering and
coordinating those tactics is separate work. These limits should guide the next
iteration, rather than turn this experiment into a promise of universal AI.

Record only useful friction: what you tried, expected result, actual result and
the smallest rule/save that reproduces it. Note whether a missing primitive,
confusing layout or uncertain timing made you want to write an Add-On. The
important outcome is whether creating and combining these mechanics is fun.
